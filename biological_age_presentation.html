Biological Age Prediction

Comparing CpGPT vs. MuLan-Methyl Foundation Clocks

Cayo Santiago Rhesus Macaque Dataset (161K RRBS Windows)

Clocks Pipeline & Methodology

1. Feature Selection

  • Started with 161,910 1kb genomic windows from Cayo Santiago dataset.
  • Applied high-dimensional **Elastic Net (alpha=0.9)** with grouped cross-validation to prevent animal-level leak.
  • Identified a sparse set of **216 top aging-predictive windows**.

2. Liftover to hg38

  • Mapped the selected coordinates from `rheMac10`/`rheMac8` to human `hg38` coordinates.
  • Successfully mapped **209 out of 216** selected CpG windows.

3. DNA Sequence Retrieval

  • Fetched hg38 sequence context from the Ensembl REST API:
    • **CpGPT**: 2001 bp context length.
    • **MuLan-Methyl**: 101 bp context length.

4. Clocks Representation

  • Extracted sequence embeddings using the foundation model encoder layers.
  • Constructed sample-level representation vector $S_i$ by weighting the window sequence embeddings with the sample's methylation level $M_{i,k}$: $$S_i = \sum M_{i,k} E_k$$
  • Evaluated all models using **Grouped 5-Fold Cross-Validation** (by `animal_id`).

Unsupervised Clock

Entropy + Variability Score

Computed as the sum of standardized variability and entropy scores across all genomic windows. Does NOT use mean or median percent methylation.

  • **High correlation** ($r = 0.300$) shows that epigenetic entropy and variability are excellent unsupervised indicators of biological age.
  • Mapped directly to the chronological age range [3, 28] years.
  • Demonstrates that aging signatures are inherently present in the structured disorder of the genome.

Elastic Net Clock

Baseline Model on 216 Features

Ridge regression clock trained on raw percent methylation values of the 216 selected windows under grouped cross-validation.

  • Achieves **exceptional accuracy** ($r = 0.975$, $R^2 = 0.950$) and MAE < 1 year.
  • Serves as the baseline for our sequence-informed clocks.
  • Confirms the 216 selected features contain very strong biological aging signals.

CpGPT Clock

SOTA Sequence-Informed Prediction

Clocks built on top of the Nucleotide Transformer sequence embeddings (2001bp context) weighted by percent methylation levels.

  • **Extremely strong performance** ($r = 0.974$, $R^2 = 0.949$), matching the base feature model.
  • Captures the context-dependent sequence patterns surrounding CpG sites.
  • Verifies that sequence embeddings carry high-fidelity epigenetic aging signals.

MuLan-Methyl Clock

Short Context Comparison

Clocks built on top of the MuLan-Methyl BERT sequence embeddings (101bp context) weighted by percent methylation levels.

  • **Poor performance** ($r = 0.424$, $R^2 = 0.180$), with an MAE of 4.08 years.
  • Suggests that short sequence contexts (101bp) are insufficient to capture complex epigenetic aging signals in a weighted representation.
  • Highlights the critical importance of larger context windows (like CpGPT's 2001bp) for downstream aging clocks.

Key Findings & Summary

  • 1. CpGPT Matches SOTA Performance
    Using sequence embeddings from the 500M parameter **Nucleotide Transformer** model (2001bp context) yielded performance ($r=0.974$, $R^2=0.949$) that is virtually identical to the optimal supervised feature clock ($r=0.975$).
  • 2. Sequence Context Length is Critical
    MuLan-Methyl (101bp context) struggled to predict age ($r=0.424$, $R^2=0.180$). This indicates that biological aging signals are distributed across a larger regulatory sequence neighborhood, which is captured by CpGPT but missed by short-context models.
  • 3. Epigenetic Disorder Reflects Chronological Age
    Our unsupervised score based solely on **variability and entropy** is highly correlated with true age ($r=0.300$), demonstrating that biological aging is fundamentally characterized by an increase in epigenetic noise and entropy.
